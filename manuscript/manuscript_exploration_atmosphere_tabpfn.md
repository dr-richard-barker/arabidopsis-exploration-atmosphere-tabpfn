## Abstract

Reduced-pressure, oxygen-enriched atmospheres are critical for future lunar and Martian habitats but impose complex stresses on plants beyond hypoxia. While oxygen enrichment restores normoxic partial pressure, reduced total pressure accelerates gas diffusivity, perturbing leaf boundary-layer mass transfer and inducing barosensory stress. Here we harmonized a multi-omics compendium of 281 microarrays, 40,860 single-nucleus transcriptomes, physiological, metabolomic, and proteomic datasets, applying the TabPFN v2 tabular foundation model to decouple hypoxia from barosensory signaling. We identified distinct molecular hubs: hypoxia-responsive genes silenced by oxygen enrichment and barosensory modules persisting under normoxia, localized to epidermal and mesophyll tissues. Physiological assays revealed increased vapor pressure deficit, stomatal closure, and reduced photosynthesis under hypobaria, which were mitigated by elevated humidity. Our findings indicate that hypobaric stress in exploration atmospheres is primarily a boundary-layer vapor-loss challenge rather than hypoxia, and that humidity compensation offers an effective strategy to maintain plant health in extraterrestrial habitats.

**Keywords:** Hypobaria; Hypoxia; *Arabidopsis thaliana*; Plant stress physiology; Space biology; Tabular foundation models.

## 1. Introduction

Long-duration crewed space exploration requires transitioning from the International Space Station (ISS) sea-level-equivalent cabin atmosphere ($97\text{--}101.3\text{ kPa}$, $21\%\ \text{O}_2$, oxygen partial pressure $p\text{O}_2 \approx 20.37\text{--}21.27\text{ kPa}$) to reduced-pressure, oxygen-enriched habitats designed to minimize structural mass, hull leakage, and pre-extravehicular activity (EVA) nitrogen denitrogenation time (Norcross et al., 2013; Paul & Ferl, 2006; Zhou et al., 2017). Specifically, NASA's **Exploration Atmosphere** engineering trade study established a primary lunar/Martian surface habitat setpoint of **$56.54\text{ kPa}$ ($8.2\text{ psia}$) total pressure at $34\%\ \text{O}_2$** ($p\text{O}_2 = 19.22\text{ kPa}$) and a staged pre-EVA decompression setpoint of **$70.33\text{ kPa}$ ($10.2\text{ psia}$) at $26.5\%\ \text{O}_2$** ($p\text{O}_2 = 18.64\text{ kPa}$) (Norcross et al., 2013). For mammalian crew physiology, raising fractional oxygen concentration ($F_{\text{I}}\text{O}_2 = 0.34$) restores alveolar $p\text{O}_2$ to near-normoxic levels. For higher plants grown in bioregenerative life-support systems, however, the biophysics of low barometric pressure introduces a thermodynamic boundary-layer acceleration that oxygen enrichment alone cannot cancel.

By the Chapman–Enskog kinetic theory of gases, the binary diffusion coefficient $D_{ij}$ of trace gases and water vapor ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, and volatile phytohormones such as ethylene) in air scales inversely with total barometric pressure:

$$D_{ij}(P_{\text{total}}, T) = D_{ij}(P_0, T) \cdot \left(\frac{P_0}{P_{\text{total}}}\right)$$

Consequently, reducing total cabin pressure from $97\text{ kPa}$ (ISS) to $56.54\text{ kPa}$ (Exploration Atmosphere) increases gas-phase molecular diffusivity by **$D/D_0 = 1.72\times$** (and by $1.94\times$ at $50\text{ kPa}$ and $3.88\times$ at $25\text{ kPa}$), thinning the effective diffusive resistance of the leaf boundary layer and accelerating cuticular and stomatal water-vapor efflux at any fixed relative humidity (Corey et al., 2002; Richards et al., 2006; Paul et al., 2004, 2017; Lucia et al., 2025). When plants are exposed to uncompensated hypobaric air ($21\%\ \text{O}_2$ at $25\text{--}75\text{ kPa}$), they experience a compound stress combining **cellular hypoxia** ($p\text{O}_2 = 5.25\text{--}15.75\text{ kPa}$, which stabilizes group VII ETHYLENE RESPONSE FACTOR [ERF-VII] transcription factors via N-degron PLANT CYSTEINE OXIDASE [PCO] inhibition; Gibbs et al., 2011; Licausi et al., 2011; Mustroph et al., 2009) with **physical barosensory stress** ($D \propto P_{\text{total}}^{-1}$, boundary-layer evaporative acceleration, and cell-wall mechanical strain; Zhou et al., 2017; Paul et al., 2017). Pioneering transcriptomic studies by Paul, Ferl, and colleagues (`GSE87867`, `GSE87868`/`OSD-136`, `GSE87903`, `GSE87904`; Zhou et al., 2017) profiled *Arabidopsis thaliana* roots and shoots across multiple total pressures ($97, 75, 50, 25, 10, 5\text{ kPa}$) and $\text{O}_2$-supplemented normoxic controls ($50\text{ kPa} / 42\%\ \text{O}_2$ and $25\text{ kPa} / 84\%\ \text{O}_2$, $p\text{O}_2 = 21.0\text{ kPa}$). Yet four critical barriers have prevented a unified systems-level synthesis:

1. **Sample Cross-Listing and Metadata Ambiguity in Public Repositories:** Across the four core GEO series (`GSE87867`, `GSE87868`, `GSE87903`, `GSE87904`), cross-listed GSM accessions, conflicting organ tags between sample titles (`Shoot`) and `characteristics_ch1` (`Root`) in `GSE87903` (`GSM2342806`–`GSM2342809`), conflicting pressure tags (`GSM2342804`–`GSM2342805`), and unexplained $n=4$ replicate groups have confounded automated meta-analyses.
2. **The Small-$n$, High-$p$ Regime in Space Biology:** Individual spaceflight and hypobaric chamber experiments typically comprise $n = 58\text{--}130$ arrays across $p = 22,746$ transcripts. Classical gradient-boosted decision trees (`LightGBM`, `XGBoost`) and deep neural networks overfit severely in this regime, while penalized linear models (`ElasticNet`, `Ridge`) fail to capture nonlinear interactions between $P_{\text{total}}$, $p\text{O}_2$, organ identity, and developmental age without explicit interaction modeling.
3. **Lack of Cellular and Single-Nucleus Spatial Resolution:** Bulk organ microarrays obscure whether barosensory ($P_{\text{total}}$) and hypoxia ($p\text{O}_2$) gene modules operate within the same cells or are spatially segregated across specialized epidermal, mesophyll, cortical, endodermal, and vascular cell lineages.
4. **Disconnect Between Transcriptomic Hubs and Whole-Plant Physiological/Metabolomic Phenotypes:** Whether the transcriptomic signatures of normoxic hypobaria translate into stomatal closure, photochemical impairment, or secondary metabolite depletion—and whether they can be rescued by habitat humidity engineering—has not been quantitatively modeled.
5. **Absence of a Unified Physiological Stress Decoding Framework:** Because spaceflight architectures involve dynamic multi-stage pressure and gas transitions (from Earth launch to habitat transit, pre-EVA staging, and emergency decompression), a standardized multi-dimensional stress projection tool is needed to decode how plants experience each phase of the astronautical mission profile.

To overcome these barriers, we leverage **TabPFN v2** (Hollmann et al., *Nature* 637, 319–326, 2025), a transformer-based tabular foundation model pre-trained on synthetic structural causal models to perform Bayesian in-context learning on small-to-medium tabular datasets ($n \le 10,000, p \le 500$), integrated with the **Plant PhysioSpace** stress decoding architecture (Esfahani et al., *Plant Physiol.* 2021). By pairing leak-free biological feature compression with TabPFN v2 and its post-hoc ensembling framework (**TabPFN v2 [PHE]**), we execute an end-to-end systems investigation across 12 multi-omics datasets (Figures 1–7). We forensically audit and harmonize the 130 unique hypobaric microarrays (WP1), decouple $P_{\text{total}}$ from $p\text{O}_2$ and identify orthogonal barosensory vs. hypoxia molecular hub networks via exact Shapley attribution (WP2), map these hubs across 19 cell-specific TRAP-seq populations and 40,860 root-tip single nuclei (WP3), bridge transcriptomic hubs to measured stomatal gas exchange, $\delta^{13}\text{C}$, LC-MS lipid/glucosinolate metabolomics, and `SnRK1.1K48M` proteomics (WP4), generate Leave-One-Condition-Out validated counterfactual predictions for NASA's $56.54\text{ kPa} / 34\%\ \text{O}_2$ Exploration Atmosphere (WP5), and decode plant stress perception across the spaceflight mission profile using the Plant PhysioSpace Stress Autodecoder (WP6).

#### Technical Re-hybridization Handling Policy
To address pseudo-replication risk, technical repeat pairs were grouped together within the same cross-validation condition group (`group_id`), preventing train–test information leakage. For downstream linear modeling and hub identification, we conducted a sensitivity analysis comparing models with technical re-hybridizations retained (grouped) versus collapsed (averaged). Gene-wise effect sizes and significance rankings remained virtually identical ($r > 0.998$ for $\beta_{\text{baro}}$ and $\beta_{\text{hypo}}$), confirming that conclusions are insensitive to technical replicate handling policy.

#### Batch and Study Confounding Assessment
Because the compendium spans multiple GEO series and scanner runs, we assessed whether experimental regimes were aliased with batch provenance. Principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated that variance is overwhelmingly dominated by biological organ identity (PC1, 62.4% of total variance) and atmospheric treatment regime (PC2, 18.1%), whereas scanner batch prefixes (`54xx` vs. `68xx`) accounted for $< 1.8\%$ of residual variance. Furthermore, in multivariable regression controlling for study series and scanner batch as covariates, all primary barosensory and hypoxic hub associations remained significant ($p < 10^{-4}$), demonstrating that atmospheric signal separation is not an artifact of batch structure.

### 2.2 Workpackage 2: TabPFN v2 Decoupling of Total Barometric Pressure ($P_{\text{total}}$) from Oxygen Partial Pressure ($p\text{O}_2$) and Orthogonal Hub Identification

Using the forensically harmonized compendium (`Table_S1_Harmonized_130_Hypobaric_Arrays_Forensic_Audit.csv`), we formulated two orthogonal physical coordinates relative to the $97\text{ kPa}$ ISS cabin control ($21\%\ \text{O}_2, p\text{O}_2 = 20.37\text{ kPa}$):
1. **Hypobaria Index (Barosensory / Diffusivity Coordinate):** $\text{HI}_{\text{baro}} = \log_2(97 / P_{\text{total}}) = \log_2(D / D_0)$, scaling from $0.000$ ($97\text{ kPa}$) to $+0.371$ ($75\text{ kPa}$), $+0.956$ ($50\text{ kPa}$), $+1.956$ ($25\text{ kPa}$), $+3.278$ ($10\text{ kPa}$), and $+4.278$ ($5\text{ kPa}$).
2. **Hypoxia Index (Oxygen Deprivation Coordinate):** $\text{HI}_{\text{hypo}} = \log_2(20.37 / p\text{O}_2)$, which is $0.000$ in normoxic air ($97\text{ kPa}$) and $-0.044$ in $\text{O}_2$-supplemented normoxic hypobaria ($50\text{ kPa} / 42\%\ \text{O}_2$ and $25\text{ kPa} / 84\%\ \text{O}_2$, $p\text{O}_2 = 21.0\text{ kPa}$), but rises to $+0.956$ in $50\text{ kPa}$ Air ($p\text{O}_2 = 10.5\text{ kPa}$), $+1.956$ in $25\text{ kPa}$ Air ($p\text{O}_2 = 5.25\text{ kPa}$), $+1.998$ in normobaric hypoxia ($97\text{ kPa} / p\text{O}_2 = 5.1\text{ kPa}$), and $+4.278$ in $5\text{ kPa}$ Air ($p\text{O}_2 = 1.05\text{ kPa}$).

Across the 58 multi-pressure and $\text{O}_2$-dissection arrays (`WP2A` and `WP2B`: `GSE87867`, `GSE87868`, `GSE87903`), empirical correlation between $\text{HI}_{\text{baro}}$ and $\text{HI}_{\text{hypo}}$ is moderate ($R^2 = 0.44$), because normoxic $\text{O}_2$-supplemented hypobaria treatments ($50\text{ kPa} + \text{O}_2$, $25\text{ kPa} + \text{O}_2$) and normobaric hypoxia ($97\text{ kPa} / 5.1\text{ kPa}\ \text{O}_2$) break collinearity between total pressure and oxygen partial pressure (Figure 1A).

Individual hypoxia hubs showed similarly universal induction (`HRU1` $+3.19\text{ to }+4.29\ \log_2\text{CPM}$; `AtPDC1` $+3.68\text{ to }+4.61\ \log_2\text{CPM}$; `AHB1` $+3.39\text{ to }+4.21\ \log_2\text{CPM}$; `ADH1` $+2.88\text{ to }+3.82\ \log_2\text{CPM}$; `Table_S4B`).
- In contrast, **`ABA_Desiccation_Stomatal_Closure` ($-0.222$ to $+0.057\ \log_2\text{CPM}$ across all 9 lineages)** and **`Cuticle_Epicuticular_Wax_Lipid_Transport` ($-0.347$ to $-0.026\ \log_2\text{CPM}$ across all 9 lineages)** were **uninduced or slightly repressed** by pure normobaric hypoxia across all 40,860 root-tip nuclei (`Table_S4A`, Figure 4C). Importantly, because `GSE308672` profiled root tips, the lack of cuticular induction reflects root anatomical specificity (cuticular wax synthesis is restricted to shoot epidermis, as confirmed by `pCER5` TRAP-seq; Figure 4A). This tissue-resolved dissociation confirms that ABA/desiccation and cuticular wax remodeling in hypobaric chambers (`GSE87867`, `GSE87903`, `GSE87904`) is an **independent barosensory ($P_{\text{total}}$) response** rather than a downstream consequence of cellular hypoxia.

## 3. Discussion and Bioregenerative Habitat Engineering Recommendations

### 3.1 Methodological Insights: Foundation Models for Small-Sample Spaceflight Omics
Spaceflight and hypobaric chamber biology exemplifies the "small-$n$, wide-$p$" regime ($n = 58\text{--}130$ samples across $p = 22,746$ genes) where standard machine learning paradigms face constraints. Tree-based ensembles (`LightGBM`, `RandomForest`) partition features via axis-aligned splits that fragment small sample sizes into low-count leaves, leading to reduced generalization on small developmental slices ($n=9$ 5-day roots) and poorer calibration (`Brier_Score = 0.5670` for `LightGBM` in WP2C; Table 2, `Table_S2B`). Conversely, penalized linear models (`ElasticNet`, `Ridge`) cannot represent conditional interactions—such as how developmental stage (`5-day` vs. `10-day`) or organ identity (`Shoot` vs. `Root`) modifies slopes—without explicit interaction engineering.

By pairing a leak-free biological feature compression layer (`TabPFNOmicsFeatureBuilder`, combining 12 curated pathway eigengenes, 8 biophysical pathway interaction ratios, and 40 unsupervised highest-variance discovery genes selected strictly inside each training fold) with **TabPFN v2** (Hollmann et al., *Nature* 2025), our framework leverages transformer in-context Bayesian inference over synthetic causal priors. This architecture provided three key capabilities:
1. **Forensic Metadata Auditing (WP1):** Strong posterior calibration (`ECE = 0.00085`, `Brier_Score = 0.000002`) allowed TabPFN v2 to identify the four disputed $75\text{ kPa}$ arrays (`GSM2342806`–`GSM2342809`) with $P(\text{Shoot}\mid\mathbf{x}) > 0.990$, which we corroborated using raw Affymetrix `.CEL` scanner records.
2. **Orthogonal Physical Decoupling (WP2):** TabPFN v2 reduced continuous prediction error (`RMSE`) by up to $54.7\%$ on $p\text{O}_2$ and $34.3\%$ on $P_{\text{total}}$ relative to `LightGBM`, and effectively distinguished normoxic hypobaria ($50\text{ kPa} + \text{O}_2$, $25\text{ kPa} + \text{O}_2$) from hypobaric hypoxia (`ROC_AUC = 0.9623`).
3. **Out-of-Condition Generalization (WP5):** In Leave-One-Condition-Out validation (`Table_S6B`), TabPFN v2 reconstructed 12-pathway profiles for held-out pressure $\times$ oxygen regimes (`mean r = 0.7387`, `RMSE = 0.1502`), facilitating counterfactual predictions at NASA's Exploration Atmosphere setpoints ($56.54\text{ kPa} / 34\%\ \text{O}_2$ and $70.33\text{ kPa} / 26.5\%\ \text{O}_2$).

### 3.2 A Unified Spatial-Mechanistic Model of Plant Adaptation to Exploration Atmospheres
Integrating our moderated orthogonal regression (`Table_S3`), cell-type TRAP-seq (`GSE14502`), 40,860-nucleus root-tip snRNA-seq (`GSE308672`), gas exchange/metabolomics (`ZENODO16536514`), and `SnRK1.1K48M` proteomics (`PXD002353`) provides a two-axis spatial framework for plant responses to reduced-pressure atmospheres:

1. **Axis 1 — The Internal Cellular Oxygen ($p\text{O}_2$) Axis:** When ambient $p\text{O}_2$ falls below $\sim 12\text{--}15\text{ kPa}$ (as in uncompensated $50\text{ kPa}$ or $25\text{ kPa}$ air), radial oxygen diffusion into dense tissues is restricted, inhibiting PCO enzymes (`PCO1`), stabilizing ERF-VII transcription factors, and driving induction of `HRU1`, `AtPDC1`, `ADH1`, `SUS1`, `AHB1`/`HB1`, `WIP5`, `AAD6`, and `FRK1` across root lineages (`Meristem_QC`, `Xylem`, `Endodermis`, `Stele_Pericycle`; `+2.65 to +3.17 log2CPM` in `GSE308672`) and cortical/vascular translatomes (`+4.60 to +5.22 log2` in `GSE14502`), with SnRK1.1 coordinating post-translational responses (`PXD002353`). **NASA's $34\%\ \text{O}_2$ enrichment at $56.54\text{ kPa}$ ($p\text{O}_2 = 19.22\text{ kPa}$) substantially relieves this hypoxia axis.**
2. **Axis 2 — The Epidermal/Mesophyll Boundary-Layer Diffusivity ($P_{\text{total}}$) Axis:** Even when $p\text{O}_2$ is maintained at $19.22\text{--}21.0\text{ kPa}$, the $1.72\times$ (at $56.54\text{ kPa}$) to $1.94\times$ (at $50\text{ kPa}$) acceleration in binary water-vapor diffusivity ($D \propto P_{\text{total}}^{-1}$) reduces boundary-layer diffusive resistance, elevating effective evaporative demand (`VPD` ratio $+73.23\%$ at $62\text{ kPa}$). This boundary-layer vapor flux promotes:
   - **Epidermal (`pCER5`) and cell-wall mechanosensory/hydrotropic responses:** `TCH4`/`AtXTH22`, `CMI1`, `EXO`, `PBP1`, `ACS6`, `LTP3`, and `LTP4` participate in cell-wall xyloglucan remodeling and cuticular barrier maintenance.
   - **Guard-cell and mesophyll (`pRBCS`) stomatal/oxylipin responses:** `STZ`/`ZAT10`, `PYL1`, `ATHB7`, `LOX2`, and `AOS` mediate stomatal closure ($g_{sw}$ $-76.25\%$) and chloroplast galactolipid (`MGDG-OPDA` / `DGDG-OPDA`) remodeling. Because PSII photochemistry (`ETR(II)`) remains functional while intercellular $\text{CO}_2$ (`ci`) is reduced by closed stomata, net carbon assimilation (`A`) decreases by **$-55.93\%$** and foliar $\delta^{13}\text{C}$ is enriched by **$+1.02\text{\textperthousand}$**.

### 3.3 Habitat Engineering and Crop-Design Recommendations
These findings inform environmental control and crop management strategies for bioregenerative life support systems operating under Exploration Atmosphere conditions ($56.54\text{ kPa} / 34\%\ \text{O}_2$):

1. **Environmental Control & Life Support System (ECLSS) Parameter — Isodiffusive Humidity Compensation ($70\text{--}75\%\ \text{RH}$):** Standard terrestrial growth chambers typically operate at $50\%\ \text{RH}$. At $56.54\text{ kPa}$ ($D/D_0 = 1.72\times$) or $62\text{ kPa}$ ($D/D_0 = 1.63\times$), $50\%\ \text{RH}$ generates boundary-layer evaporative flux comparable to atmospheric drought, inducing stomatal closure ($g_{sw} = 8.70\ \text{mmol m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$) and reducing photosynthetic carbon assimilation. To offset the accelerated evaporation rate $E \propto D(P_{\text{total}}) \cdot (1 - \text{RH})$, plant growth chambers operating at $56.54\text{--}62\text{ kPa}$ should consider maintaining **relative humidity at $70\text{--}75\%\ \text{RH}$** (reducing vapor pressure deficit by $\sim 40\text{--}50\%$). As shown in `ZENODO16536514` (`Table_S5B`, Figure 5C), raising humidity to $70\%\ \text{RH}$ at $62\text{ kPa}$ restores chloroplast `MGDG-OPDA`/`DGDG-OPDA` galactolipids ($+1.26\text{ to }+2.89\ \log_2\text{FC}$) and thioalkyl glucosinolates ($+0.68\ \log_2\text{FC}$) while moderating stress flavonols toward baseline.
2. **Diagnostic Panel & Genetic Targets:** For real-time monitoring of crop status in lunar/Martian habitats, our orthogonal hub decomposition (`Table_S3`, `Table_S6A`) outlines a **10-gene diagnostic reporter panel** distinguishing **Chamber $\text{O}_2$ / Root Aeration Status** (`HRU1`, `AtPDC1`, `AHB1`/`HB1`, `PCO1`, `WIP5`) from **Boundary-Layer Diffusive / Barosensory Stress** (`TCH4`/`AtXTH22`, `CMI1`, `STZ`/`ZAT10`, `ACS6`, `LOX2`). In addition, modulating guard-cell ABA sensitivity (`STZ`/`ZAT10`, `HAI1`) while maintaining epidermal cuticular barrier reinforcement (`pCER5:LTP3/CER1`) and cell-wall resilience (`TCH4`) represents a potential target for preserving stomatal conductance and growth under reduced pressure.

### 3.4 Contextualizing Spaceflight Stress: From Hypoxia to Boundary-Layer Dynamics
For decades, spaceflight plant experiments and hypobaric chamber simulations have conflated low pressure with low oxygen (Corey et al., 1997; Paul et al., 2004, 2006; Richards et al., 2006). Because Earth-analog hypobaric chambers frequently pumped down ambient air without oxygen enrichment, historical studies observed dramatic inductions of alcohol dehydrogenase (*ADH1*), pyruvate decarboxylase (*PDC1*), and non-symbiotic hemoglobins (*GLB1*/*AHB1*), cementing the widespread dogma that hypobaria is fundamentally a hypoxic stress (Paul et al., 2004; Zhou et al., 2017).

Our deployment of TabPFN v2 orthogonal decomposition alongside the Plant PhysioSpace Stress Autodecoder directly overturns this dogma for operational spaceflight architectures. Under NASA's nominal Exploration Atmosphere ($56.54\text{ kPa} / 34\%\ \text{O}_2$), the oxygen partial pressure ($19.22\text{ kPa}$) is virtually identical to sea-level terrestrial air ($21.27\text{ kPa}$). Consequently, internal cellular hypoxia is strongly suppressed ($z = -0.16$ in shoots; Figure 7A). Instead, plants experience reduced atmospheric pressure as an acute boundary-layer vapor-loss stress caused by the kinetic acceleration of binary gas diffusion ($D \propto P_{\text{total}}^{-1}$). Major molecular challenges faced by plants in exploration habitats include cuticular micro-dehydration, cell-wall mechanotension (*TCH4*, *XTH15*, *MSL8*), and stomatal closure (*SLAC1*, *OST1*, *PYL1*), which reduce mesophyll internal $\text{CO}_2$ and substantially decrease photosynthetic carbon assimilation.

Importantly, this perspective points toward environmental management in Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLiSS): maintaining chamber relative humidity at **$75\text{--}80\%\ \text{RH}$** (Figure 7C–D) attenuates the elevated boundary-layer VPD, mitigating stomatal restriction and supporting stable carbon assimilation in reduced-pressure exploration habitats.

### 4.2 Leak-Free Biological Feature Compression and TabPFN v2 Modeling Pipeline (WP1 & WP2)
Because high-dimensional microarray matrices ($p = 22,746 \gg n = 58\text{--}130$) exceed the optimal feature capacity of tabular transformers ($p \le 500$) and risk data leakage if feature selection is performed globally, we implemented `TabPFNOmicsFeatureBuilder` (`src/atmos_ml/features.py`), a scikit-learn transformer fit **strictly inside each cross-validation training fold**. Inside `fit(X_train, y_train)`, `TabPFNOmicsFeatureBuilder` constructs a compact feature representation comprising:
1. **12 Curated Mechanistic Pathway Eigengenes:** Mean $z$-scored expression (fit using training-fold mean and SD only) across 12 biological modules (`ERF_VII_Core_Hypoxia_Anaerobic`, `ABA_Desiccation_Stomatal_Closure`, `Cuticle_Epicuticular_Wax_Lipid_Transport`, `Cell_Wall_Mechanosensing_Expansion`, `Oxylipin_JA_Chloroplast_Galactolipid`, `Indole_Glucosinolate_Defense`, `Aliphatic_Glucosinolate_Biosynthesis`, `Phenylpropanoid_Sinapate_Flavonol`, `SnRK1_Starvation_Sugar_Autophagy`, `Photosynthesis_Carbon_Fixation_LHC`, `ROS_Redox_Calcium_HeatShock`, and `Ethylene_Auxin_Root_Remodeling`).
2. **8 Biophysical Pathway Interaction Ratios:** Domain-specific difference scores capturing orthogonality between hypoxia and barosensory axes (e.g., `INT_ABA_minus_Hypoxia`, `INT_Cuticle_minus_Hypoxia`, `INT_CellWall_minus_Hypoxia`, `INT_Indole_minus_Aliphatic_GSL`, `INT_Oxylipin_minus_Photosynthesis`).
3. **Top $K = 40$ Unsupervised High-Variance Discovery Probesets:** Selected strictly from `X_train` variance (excluding curated pathway genes to prevent collinearity) and standardized using `X_train` statistics.
4. **Known Experimental Covariates:** Binary organ indicator (`COV_is_shoot`) and seedling age in days (`COV_age_days`) for atmospheric prediction tasks.

We benchmarked **TabPFN v2** (`tabpfn.TabPFNClassifier` and `tabpfn.TabPFNRegressor`, v2.2.1; Hollmann et al., *Nature* 2025, `n_estimators=8`) and **TabPFN v2 (PHE)** (post-hoc ensemble averaging predictions across random seeds `[42, 101, 2025]` and temperatures `[0.75, 0.9, 1.0]`, weighted by inverse validation loss) against `LightGBM` (`HistGradientBoostingRegressor`/`Classifier`), `RandomForest`, `ElasticNet`, `Ridge`, `MLP`, and `Dummy` baselines (`src/atmos_ml/models.py`). Cross-validation used condition-aware `GroupKFold` using 20 discrete condition groups defined by `f"{series_id}_{consensus_organ}_{age_days}d_{duration_h}h_{P_total_kPa}kPa_{O2_frac}O2"`, ensuring technical re-hybridization pairs were never split across train and test folds. All metrics (`RMSE`, `MAE`, $R^2$, Spearman $\rho$, `ROC_AUC`, `PR_AUC`, `Macro_F1`, `Accuracy`, `Brier_Score`, `ECE`) were accompanied by **1,000-iteration bootstrap 95% confidence intervals** and two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-values (`Table_S2A`, `Table_S2B`).

### 4.3 Multivariable Orthogonal Partial Regression with Empirical Bayes Moderation (WP2)
To decouple $P_{\text{total}}$ from $p\text{O}_2$ across all 22,746 biological probesets, we fit multivariable linear models across the 58 multi-pressure/$\text{O}_2$-dissection arrays controlling for organ and developmental age:

$$y_{ig} = \beta_{0g} + \beta_{\text{baro},g}\log_2\!\left(\frac{97}{P_{\text{total},i}}\right) + \beta_{\text{hypo},g}\log_2\!\left(\frac{20.37}{p\text{O}_2,i}\right) + \beta_{\text{organ},g}\mathbb{I}(\text{Shoot}_i) + \beta_{\text{age},g}\text{Age}_i + \varepsilon_{ig}$$

To stabilize residual variance estimates across small sample sizes, we applied limma empirical Bayes variance moderation:

$$\tilde{s}_g^2 = \frac{d_0 s_0^2 + d_g s_g^2}{d_0 + d_g}$$

where prior degrees of freedom $d_0 = 1.76$ and prior variance $s_0^2 = 0.1188$ were estimated from the empirical distribution of residual variances. Moderated $t$-statistics were tested with Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction (`Table_S3_Full_22746_Orthogonal_Decomposition.csv`). In addition, we fit an interaction model incorporating $\beta_{\text{int},g}(\text{HI}_{\text{baro}}\times\text{HI}_{\text{hypo}})$ to classify synergistic ($\beta_{\text{int}} > 0, p < 0.05$) and antagonistic ($\beta_{\text{int}} < 0, p < 0.05$) dual hubs. For non-additive feature attribution inside **TabPFN v2**, we used `shap.Explainer` (`PermutationExplainer` with `max_evals = 2 * n_features + 1`) on the fitted `TabPFNRegressor` models (`src/atmos_ml/interpret.py`).

### 4.4 Cell-Type TRAP-seq (`GSE14493`, `GSE14502`) and Single-Nucleus RNA-seq (`GSE308672`) Processing (WP3)
For `GSE14493` ($n=12$) and `GSE14502` ($n=87$), RMA-normalized $\log_2$ expression matrices were mapped to the 12 curated pathways and top 80 molecular hubs (`Table_S4A`, `Table_S4B`), computing baseline cell-type localization ($\Delta\log_2$ of each promoter line under normoxia minus global normoxic mean across all lines) and cell-type hypoxia induction ($\Delta\log_2\text{FC}$ hypoxia minus normoxia within each promoter line). For `GSE308672` (`GSM9251057`–`GSM9251060`), we ingested four 10x Genomics count matrices, filtered barcodes to **40,860 high-quality nuclei** ($\ge 200$ detected genes and $\ge 300$ UMIs per nucleus), normalized counts to $\log_2(\text{CPM} + 1)$, assigned each nucleus to one of 9 canonical root-tip lineages (`Stele_Pericycle`, `Phloem`, `Xylem`, `Endodermis`, `Cortex`, `Epidermis_Atrichoblast`, `Epidermis_Trichoblast`, `Columella_RootCap`, `Meristem_QC`), and aggregated pseudobulk expression at the `sample × lineage` level (`Table_S4A`, `Table_S4B`).

### 4.5 Physiological, Metabolomic (`ZENODO16536514`), and Proteomic (`PXD002353`) Analyses (WP4)
From `ZENODO16536514`, we analyzed whole-plant gas exchange (`gas exchange.xlsx`, $n=52$), PAM chlorophyll fluorescence light-response curves (`chl fluorescence.xlsx`, $n=252$), leaf fresh/dry weight and relative water content (`RWC.xlsx`, $n=14$), foliar $\delta^{13}\text{C}$ and nitrogen content (`C_N_13C.xlsx`, $n=14$), pigment ratios (`Pigments.xlsx`, $n=20$), and LC-MS secondary metabolites and galactolipids (`Secondary metabolites.xlsx`, 28 compounds across 4 pressure $\times$ humidity groups). Gas exchange measurements were acquired using a Li-6800 portable photosynthesis system under controlled environmental conditions: leaf temperature $22.0 \pm 0.2^\circ\text{C}$, reference $\text{CO}_2$ concentration $400\ \mu\text{mol mol}^{-1}$, chamber air flow $500\ \mu\text{mol s}^{-1}$, and photosynthetic photon flux density ($PPFD$) of $150\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$ for steady-state surveys. The mole-fraction leaf-to-air vapor pressure deficit was calculated as $VPD_{\text{mol}} = (e_{\text{sat}}(T_{\text{leaf}}) - e_{\text{air}})/P_{\text{total}}$. Chlorophyll fluorescence light curves ($0\text{--}1092\ \mu\text{mol m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$) were fit using a non-rectangular hyperbola model to extract maximum electron transport rate ($ETR_{\max}$) and light saturation points. LC-MS secondary metabolites were identified at Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) Level 2 based on accurate mass ($\Delta m/z < 5\text{ ppm}$) and MS/MS fragmentation matching against authentic reference spectra. Both parametric (`Mean`, `SD`, Welch's $t$-test $p$, Hedges' $g$) and non-parametric (`Median`, `IQR`, Mann–Whitney $U$ $p$) statistics were computed for every trait (`Table_S5A`, `Table_S5B`). For `PXD002353`, we parsed iTRAQ spreadsheets (5,454 unique AGI proteins) comparing `Col-0` and `SnRK1.1K48M` rosettes across $0, 0.5, 1,$ and $3\text{ h}$ of submergence, integrating $\log_2$ protein ratios with our 283 hub nodes (`Table_S5C`).

### 4.6 Leave-One-Condition-Out Validation and Conformal Counterfactual Synthesis (WP5)
To evaluate out-of-condition generalization across $(P_{\text{total}}, p\text{O}_2)$ space, we trained multi-output response-surface models mapping physical coordinates $[\text{HI}_{\text{baro}}, \text{HI}_{\text{hypo}}, D/D_0, F_{\text{I}}\text{O}_2, \mathbb{I}_{\text{Shoot}}, \text{Age}, \text{HI}_{\text{baro}}\times\mathbb{I}_{\text{Shoot}}, \text{HI}_{\text{hypo}}\times\mathbb{I}_{\text{Shoot}}]$ to differential expression ($\Delta\log_2\text{FC}$ vs. matched $97\text{ kPa}$ control) across the 12 pathways and key hub genes. Leave-One-Condition-Out (LOCO) cross-validation was performed by iteratively holding out each of the 10 $(P_{\text{total}}, p\text{O}_2) \times \text{Organ}$ condition regimes (`Table_S6B`). Split-conformal prediction intervals were calibrated on LOCO residuals:

$$\hat{C}_{1-\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) = \left[ \hat{y}(\mathbf{x}) - q_{1-\alpha}, \hat{y}(\mathbf{x}) + q_{1-\alpha} \right]$$

where $q_{0.90} = 0.3007\ \log_2\text{FC}$ and $q_{0.95} = 0.3289\ \log_2\text{FC}$ represent the empirical 90th and 95th percentiles of out-of-condition absolute prediction errors (`Table_S6A`).

### 4.7 Plant PhysioSpace Stress Autodecoder (WP6)
The Plant PhysioSpace Stress Autodecoder projects high-dimensional transcriptomic profiles onto a standardized coordinate system spanning 10 physiological stress dimensions (Esfahani et al., *Plant Physiol.* 2021). Stress scores are computed as standardized z-scores relative to terrestrial ground control:

$$z_{s} = \frac{\mathbf{w}_s^T (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_0)}{\sigma_s}$$

where $\mathbf{w}_s$ represents the characteristic projection vector for stress dimension $s$, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_0$ denotes the terrestrial baseline mean, and $\sigma_s$ represents the standard deviation of projection scores across reference datasets. Trajectories across mission phases M1–M6 were plotted using a diverging Blue-to-White-to-Red colormap.
